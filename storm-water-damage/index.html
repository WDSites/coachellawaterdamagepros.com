Storms are rare in the Coachella Valley, which is exactly why they cause so much damage when they arrive. Roofs, windows and drains built for a dry climate meet a heavy downpour, and water finds its way indoors. This page covers storm water damage inside a home, meaning rain that came through the building envelope, in Coachella, CA.

Roofers repair the roof and plumbers fix lines. Our crews handle the water, the wet materials and the drying inside. Storm water that rises from streets, drains or the ground is a different loss, and flood damage restoration covers it.

How Storms Reach Coachella Homes

The city averages about 5 inches of rain a year. NASA Earth Observatory described the August 2023 rain from Tropical Storm Hilary in nearby Palm Springs as about six months of rain in six hours. In October 2026, the National Weather Service recorded 1.71 inches at Thermal from the remnants of Tropical Storm Rachel, according to local station KESQ.

A local roofer told KESQ that flat roofs held ponded water, tile roof underlayment leaked, and drains and gutters clogged with palm and tamarisk debris. Swamp coolers dripping onto roof surfaces were also named as a cause. Some repairs made after Hilary failed early, which is a reminder that a patch needs to be done properly.

Water also enters through stucco cracks, window and door frames, garage doors and openings where a roof meets a wall. Wind-driven rain pushes water sideways into places that normally stay dry. Once inside, it follows framing, soaks insulation and drops onto ceilings.

What You See and What It Means

  • A new stain or a drip on a ceiling after rain means water got past the roof
  • A bubbling ceiling or a sag means water is collecting above the drywall
  • Wet carpet or tile near an exterior door or window means water came in at the frame
  • Damp insulation in an attic or a wall cavity stays wet far longer than the room below
  • Mud or debris on a floor means runoff entered the house
  • A musty smell after the storm points to moisture inside the walls or ceiling

If the water carried mud, debris or sewage, treat it as contaminated and read the flood damage restoration page for how that changes the work.

Safety Steps for the First Hour

  1. Stay out of rooms with a sagging or bulging ceiling, because wet drywall is heavy and can fall.
  2. Leave breakers alone unless the panel is in a dry spot, and then switch off only the wet rooms.
  3. Stay away from downed power lines, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical risk.
  4. Keep children and pets out of water that carries mud or debris.
  5. Place buckets and plastic under drips and move valuables out of the drip line.
  6. Photograph the damage and the roof from the ground, then call the 24/7 line.

Do not climb a wet roof

A wet roof is slippery and dangerous. Cover and inspect it only after the weather clears, and leave the roof work to a roofer.

Insurance After a Storm

The California Department of Insurance notes that homeowners and commercial policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. Rain that enters through a storm-damaged roof is often treated differently from water that rises from the ground. Ask your insurer which one your loss falls under.

Flood coverage comes from the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood policy, and an NFIP policy takes effect 30 days after purchase in most cases. The CDI also notes that the California FAIR Plan does not cover storm-related damage without a separate policy. We provide photos and readings, and your insurer decides coverage.

Mistakes to Avoid After a Storm

  • Climbing a wet roof to look for the leak
  • Painting over a ceiling stain before the roof is repaired and the cavity is dry
  • Leaving wet insulation in an attic or wall
  • Waiting to photograph the damage until after cleanup
  • Treating mud and runoff like clean water

Storm Water Damage Questions in Coachella

Is emergency storm water damage cleanup available in Coachella?

Yes. Calls are answered during and after storms, day or night. Say where the rain came in and what is wet, and the dispatcher explains what to do first. A crew is sent the same day when it can reach you, which can depend on road conditions.

Do you repair the roof?

No. Roof repair belongs to a roofer. We extract the water, dry the structure and rebuild the interior that the water damaged.

Is storm water from a roof the same as flood water?

No. Rain that came through a roof is usually treated as cleaner than runoff that picked up mud and debris. A technician checks what the water carried and explains the difference in the work.

Will my insurance cover storm damage?

It depends on the policy and the cause. Rain through a storm-damaged roof is often treated differently from rising water, and flood damage usually needs a flood policy. Ask your insurer which applies.

Should I wait for the storm to end before calling?

No. Stop the water where you safely can, then call. Wet materials start absorbing water right away, and crews can plan while conditions are being sorted out.
