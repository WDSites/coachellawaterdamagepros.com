Water in a commercial building is a different problem from water in a house. Floors are larger, ceilings are often suspended panels, walls are shared and an owner or manager is usually coordinating with an insurer, a landlord and sometimes neighboring spaces. This page covers what our crews can do for a commercial water loss in Coachella, CA.

Our work is water extraction, structural drying and documentation. We do not repair plumbing or roofs, and we do not promise response times. For a loss that involves standing water across large areas, emergency water removal covers the extraction step.

How Commercial Losses Differ

Commercial spaces tend to have large open floors, shared walls and ceilings, and flooring systems such as carpet tile and sealed concrete. Water spreads farther before anyone notices, especially overnight or over a weekend. A leak above a suspended ceiling can soak insulation and tiles without any visible sign.

Our own address is in a multi-tenant plaza on Cesar Chavez Street, which shows how common shared buildings are in the city. When walls, ceilings and roofs are shared, one failure can affect more than one space. The first questions are who owns the building, who holds the lease and who carries the insurance.

State Route 86 runs through the area as a four-lane expressway, and the buildings along it vary in age, construction and ownership. We assess what the water reached and explain the scope before work starts. We do not name industries or clients here, because every loss is treated on its own facts.

What You See and What It Means

  • Stained or sagging ceiling tiles mean water is collecting above the grid
  • Carpet tile that lifts or curls at the seams means water reached the adhesive
  • Wet drywall at the base of a shared wall can mean water moved from the next space
  • A damp smell in a closed office or storage area points to hidden moisture
  • Standing water in a mechanical or utility room needs attention before it spreads
  • Rising readings on a meter in a wall that looked dry suggest water inside the cavity

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show where water traveled, including above ceilings and behind wall finishes.

Safety Steps for the First Hour

  1. Move people out of any area with a sagging ceiling, standing water near electrical equipment or a slippery floor.
  2. Shut off the water supply to the affected area or the building if you know where the valve is.
  3. Cut power to wet areas only if the panel is in a dry spot you can reach safely.
  4. Tell the building owner or manager and your insurer right away.
  5. Photograph the damage, the source and affected inventory before anything is moved.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and describe the size of the area and the materials wet.

Electrical and fire systems

If water has reached a panel, a server room or fire protection equipment, keep people away and call 911 or the utility. Report a discharged sprinkler line to the building manager and the fire department.

How We Handle a Commercial Water Loss

1

Assess

Moisture meters and thermal imaging map the wet areas on floors, walls and above ceilings.

2

Extract

Extraction units remove standing water, and wet carpet, pad and ceiling materials that cannot dry are removed.

3

Dry

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry floors, walls and cavities, and readings track progress.

4

Document

Photos, moisture logs and a written scope are saved so the owner and the insurer have the same record.

The published standard, ANSI/IICRC S500, applies to commercial buildings as it does to homes, and it ties the end of drying to measured readings.

Insurance, Landlords and Lease Questions

The California Department of Insurance notes that commercial property policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow, and that flood coverage is separate. Water from a sudden plumbing or roof failure is a different kind of loss. Read the policy or ask the insurer which one applies.

Some commercial policies also cover lost income after a covered loss, and the wording varies widely. Who pays for what often depends on the lease, so ask the owner and the insurer who handles which part. We provide readings and photos for whoever needs them, and the insurer decides coverage.

Mistakes to Avoid After a Commercial Water Loss

  • Waiting for a convenient time to start drying
  • Leaving wet ceiling tiles or insulation in place
  • Moving inventory without photographing the damage
  • Assuming a neighbor's wall is dry because it looks normal
