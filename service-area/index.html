Where We Work

Coachella and Four Nearby Towns

The home base is Coachella, ZIP 92236, in Riverside County. From there the same emergency line serves Indio to the northwest, Thermal and Mecca to the southeast and La Quinta to the west. Distances in the table are rough straight-line estimates, so allow for traffic and for the route that crews can actually take on the day.

The permit column matters after the water is dry. Indio, La Quinta and Coachella are cities with their own building offices. Thermal and Mecca are unincorporated communities, so permits for repair work there go through the county. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the building office before you start rebuilding.

Government offices are another reason the table matters. City and county permit offices keep weekday hours, and the City of Coachella Building Division and Utilities Department do not open on Fridays, so a water-main or sewer problem that starts on a Friday goes to the utility's standby line listed on the city website rather than to the front counter. Our line stays open throughout.

Local Detail

What Differs From Town to Town

Each town page covers the conditions that shape water damage there. Indio is the largest neighbor and the one with the most apartments and rental homes near its event grounds. Thermal mixes farmland and large rural properties, so water often has a long way to travel before anyone notices it.

Mecca sits on the north shore of the Salton Sea and has the driest climate and the lowest elevation of the five. La Quinta lies against the Santa Rosa Mountains, where runoff from the slopes is the storm concern. If you are near a town line, read both pages.

Water damage in this part of the valley follows the same pattern everywhere. Leaks from cooling systems, supply lines and appliances are the everyday cause, and storms are the occasional one. What changes is the building stock, the distance from the nearest crew and the office that issues permits, which is why the tables and pages here separate them.

Not on the list?

Calls from nearby communities are welcome, and whether a crew can reach you depends on where crews are working that day. Call the 24/7 line, give your address and ZIP, and you will hear straight away whether the job can be taken.

One practical tip applies everywhere. Before you call, write down your street address and ZIP code and note whether the water is clean or came from a sewer or storm drain. Those two facts tell us which safety steps to give you first and which crew to send.

Before You Rebuild

Checking Flood Risk and Permits for Your Address

Storm water is the one cause that most policies treat differently. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you look up an address and see how the map treats it, and the California Department of Insurance notes that homeowners policies typically exclude flood. That is worth knowing before a storm, not after one.

For repair permits, ask the office in the table above. The City of Coachella Building Division handles remodel and repair permits inside city limits and keeps weekday hours that run Monday to Thursday, so a Friday question may wait until the next week. For Thermal and Mecca, ask Riverside County Building and Safety.
