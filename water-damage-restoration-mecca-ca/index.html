Rain, heat and harvest: Mecca's water seasons

Mecca has a hot arid climate with an average of about 3.05 inches of rain a year and a record high of 126 degrees. In summer, air conditioners work for long hours and condensate lines clog or overflow. A damp patch on a ceiling or closet wall in July is often the first sign, so check the line before the stain grows.

Harvest brings up to 5,000 seasonal farmworkers to the area, and about 49 percent of residents work in agriculture. Busy homes run washers, water heaters and sinks harder, which adds wear to hoses and fittings. Owners and landlords can replace old washing machine hoses ahead of the season and show tenants where the shutoff valves are.

Storms the east valley still remembers

From 1905 to 1907, Colorado River floodwater poured into the Salton Sink and created the Salton Sea. It came close to Mecca but never reached it. A hurricane in 1976 and floods in the 1980s later slowed the progress of the eastern valley, a reminder that low ground collects water.

In October 2026, remnants of Tropical Storm Rachel left streets in nearby North Shore buried in mud. North Shore sits close to Mecca but is not on our listed coverage, so call with the address and we say plainly whether we can help. For your own address, check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center rather than guessing.

Farm country, canals and ground that holds water

Groundwater and Coachella Canal water turned this part of the Colorado Desert into farmland, and date palms have been farmed near Mecca since the late 1890s. That history means irrigation lines, ditches and wet fields are never far from a home. A broken irrigation line that runs against a wall is outside water, so keep out of it and call before you start cleaning.

Mecca has an elementary school but no public high school, and the Farmworker Service Center, the Mecca Fire Station and a Boys and Girls Club unit serve the community. If you cannot give a street number, naming one of these places helps the dispatcher place you. If Water Is Still Spreading in Mecca

Work through this list while the crew is on the way, and stop if any step looks unsafe.

Find the valve

Close the fixture valve or the main valve. For a broken irrigation line, shut the line's own valve as well.

Mind the electrics

Turn off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. Otherwise stay out and call the utility.

Raise belongings

Lift boxes, bedding and appliances off the floor and open closet doors so air can move.

Stay out of dirty water

Storm runoff, sewage and irrigation overflow can carry contamination, so keep children and pets away.

Does a hot, dry climate mean less mold after a leak?

No. Mold needs moisture, and a leak that stays wet in a wall or closet can support it even in the desert. Fix the source, dry the area quickly, and see our page on mold remediation if wet material was left in place.
