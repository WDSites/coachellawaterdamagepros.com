Where Pueblo Viejo Sits

Pueblo Viejo is the name the City of Coachella uses for its historic downtown core. The municipal code outlines the district, for one set of its provisions, as the area bounded by Cesar Chavez Street on the south, First Street on the west, Grapefruit Boulevard on the north and Ninth Street on the east.

That outline appears in a specific section of the code, so treat it as a working description and not as an official neighborhood line. If your address is near an edge, tell the dispatcher your cross streets and the answer is simple.

What Kinds of Buildings Are Here

The city's downtown zones are written to guide a higher-density, walkable mix of homes and storefronts around Veteran's Park and City Hall. The code permits multi-family buildings of five or more units, existing single-family homes built before July 1, 2019, accessory dwelling units and, under conditions, first-floor homes in mixed-use buildings.

One recent example is Pueblo Viejo Villas on 6th Street, near the library and City Hall. KESQ reported in May 2022 that it opened with about 105 family units of one, two and three bedrooms.

How Water Travels Between Units

In a building where homes share ceilings, walls and plumbing chases, a leak rarely stays where it started. A split supply line in one kitchen can wet the ceiling of the unit below and the wall of the unit next door. Moisture mapping across neighboring spaces matters more than the stain you can see.

Drying often needs access to more than one unit. That makes the property manager or owner part of the plan from the first call, and it is why a written report to them should go out the same day you find the leak.

Who Does What After a Leak

  • Tenants report the leak to the manager or owner in writing, shut off any fixture valve they can reach, and photograph the damage
  • Owners and managers close the building or unit shutoff, notify neighbors who may be affected and arrange access
  • Insurers decide coverage, so ask yours and your landlord's carrier what each policy handles
  • The City Building Division, at 53990 Enterprise Way, handles plan check, permits and inspection for repair work, and its hours run Monday through Thursday

Renters should not assume the landlord's policy covers belongings. Coverage depends on the policy and the cause of the loss, and we do not promise any outcome from a claim.

Safety Steps Before Anyone Arrives

  1. Turn off the water at the fixture or unit shutoff if you can reach it without stepping into water.
  2. Do not operate breakers or switches while standing in water. Call 911 or the electric utility if water reaches a panel.
  3. Keep out from under a ceiling that bulges or drips heavily.
  4. Move small items and electronics to dry ground and photograph everything first.
  5. If the water came from a toilet or sewer line, keep people away and do not try to clean it up with household tools.

Storms, Floods and Insurance

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The City of Coachella directs residents to the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to check an address, and that lookup is the right place to start.

The California Department of Insurance notes that homeowners and commercial policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. Flood cover comes from the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood policy, and a new NFIP policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase.

Questions From Pueblo Viejo Property Owners and Renters

Is Pueblo Viejo the same as downtown Coachella?

The city uses Pueblo Viejo for its historic downtown core, and its code defines the district between Cesar Chavez Street, First Street, Grapefruit Boulevard and Ninth Street for one set of provisions. Give your cross streets when you call so there is no guesswork.

A leak started in the apartment above mine. What should I do?

Tell your property manager in writing right away, photograph the water and the damage, and move belongings out of the wet area. Drying usually has to include the unit above, so access and coordination with the manager come first.

Who pays for water damage in a rental unit?

It depends on the cause, the lease and the policies involved. Ask the property owner and your renters insurer. We document moisture readings and photos, and the insurer decides coverage.

Do you work in mixed-use buildings with shops below apartments?

Yes, the drying approach is the same, but each space may have a different owner and policy. Call with the address and which floors are wet.

Is my address in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here. Enter your address in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, which the City of Coachella points residents to.
