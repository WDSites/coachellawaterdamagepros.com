If water is still coming in or standing deep, start with emergency water removal. If the mess is gone but the structure is wet, drying belongs to water mitigation. This page is about everything in between: what gets hauled out, what gets cleaned and how the house is left.

When the mess is spreading or smells wrong, treat it as urgent

Wet material does not wait. Carpet pad soaks up water like a sponge, drywall paper starts to bubble, and dirt or sewage residue dries into a film that is harder to remove later. Items that sat in contaminated water are usually discarded, so the sooner they are sorted, the fewer other things they touch.

We take emergency cleanup calls at night and on weekends and offer same-day response when available. We do not promise an arrival window, but we will tell you what to set aside and what to leave alone until a crew has looked at it.

Safety first with contaminated water

Water from a toilet, a sewer line, a flooded yard or a drain is not safe to handle barehanded. Keep children and pets out, wear waterproof gloves and boots if you must enter, and do not run a household vacuum on wet floors.

If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out and call 911 or the utility before you call us. If a sewer overflow comes from the city main rather than your own plumbing, the City of Coachella Utilities Department lists a separate sewer standby line on its website.

Water categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sorts water into clean, gray and black. Ask any company you hire whether its crews work to that standard, and ask how it decided which category your water fell into.

Odor: remove the source, do not mask it

A musty or sour smell after water damage almost always comes from wet material left in place, such as pad, insulation or the bottom of a wall. Sprays can cover the smell for a day but the source keeps going. We remove the source first, clean the surfaces, and use HEPA air scrubbers and deodorizing treatments only after that.

If a smell returns after cleanup, tell us. It can mean there is still moisture in a cavity, and that is a question for a moisture reading, not for another spray.

Wet belongings and contents

Photograph everything before you move it, then separate hard, nonporous items such as glass, metal and plastic from porous ones such as cardboard, soft furniture and paper. Hard items can usually be cleaned and sanitized. Porous items that touched dirty water are usually discarded, and your photos are the record your insurer will want.

Water Damage Cleanup Questions

Do you handle water damage cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Coachella?

Yes. The line is answered 24 hours a day, and we offer same-day response when available. We do not promise an exact arrival time. When you call, we will tell you what to move, what to photograph and what to leave in place until a crew has seen it.

Can I clean up water damage myself?

Small clean-water spills on hard floors, yes. Anything involving a toilet, sewer, drain, flood or mud, or any wet carpet and drywall, is better left to a crew, because hidden moisture and contamination are easy to miss and mistakes cost more later.

What is the difference between cleanup and mitigation?

Cleanup removes water, debris and ruined materials and sanitizes and deodorizes what stays. Mitigation focuses on drying the structure with equipment and moisture readings. Many jobs need both, and they are written as separate parts of the scope.

How do I know if the water is gray or black?

Look at the source. Water from a toilet bowl with waste, a sewer line, a drain or a flooded yard is not clean. Gray water comes from sources like washing machines and dishwashers, and black water carries serious contamination. A technician will classify it on site against the S500 water categories.

What should I photograph before cleanup starts?

Take wide shots of each room, close shots of damaged flooring, walls and ceilings, and photos of damaged belongings. Include a date if you can. Photos plus moisture readings give your insurer a clear record.

Do you remove the debris?

Yes. Removal of ruined materials is part of the scope, and it is written into the scope before work starts so you know what is leaving the house.
